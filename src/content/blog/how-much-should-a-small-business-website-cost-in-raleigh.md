A small business website in Raleigh costs anywhere from almost nothing for a do-it-yourself builder to well over ten thousand dollars for a custom agency build. The right figure depends on the path you choose and on what you keep paying after launch. Comparing the cost over three to five years tells you as much as the first invoice does, and it is the comparison most owners skip.

This breakdown covers each common option, what it includes, and how to compare totals fairly.

## What Are the Four Ways to Get a Small Business Website?

### 1. Do-it-yourself builders

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, and GoDaddy's builder let you assemble a site from templates.

* **Upfront cost:** often $0 to start
* **Monthly cost:** roughly $16 to $50 for a business plan, billed as long as the site is live
* **Your time:** significant, especially the first time
* **Best fit:** a new business that needs a simple presence fast and has more time than budget

The tradeoffs are performance and ceiling. Template builders load more code than most small sites need, which affects speed and can hold back local rankings. When you outgrow the template, your options for customizing the design are limited, and moving to another platform means rebuilding the site.

### 2. Freelancers

An independent designer or developer builds a custom or semi-custom site.

* **Upfront cost:** commonly $1,500 to $6,000 for a small business site in the Triangle
* **Monthly cost:** hosting and maintenance, often $20 to $150
* **Timeline:** three to eight weeks

Quality and availability vary widely. Confirm who hosts the site, who makes updates, and what those updates cost.

### 3. Agencies (one-time build)

An agency handles strategy, design, development, and often content.

* **Upfront cost:** commonly $5,000 to $25,000 and up for a small business site
* **Monthly cost:** a care plan, often $50 to $500
* **Timeline:** two to four months

You get a polished result and a team. The considerations are the size of the upfront commitment and the redesign cycle, since many agency sites are rebuilt every three to five years.

### 4. Monthly plans

Some providers build a custom site with little or no upfront cost and charge a flat monthly fee that covers the build, hosting, and ongoing changes.

* **Upfront cost:** $0 down is common
* **Monthly cost:** typically $100 to $300, which folds in hosting, security, and edits
* **Timeline:** two to six weeks

## What Drives the Price of a Small Business Website?

Whatever path you choose, these factors move the cost most:

* **Number of pages.** A five-page site is far less work than a thirty-page site with dozens of service and location pages.
* **Custom design versus template.** A design built for your brand takes longer than adjusting a stock theme.
* **Content.** Writing copy and sourcing photos adds hours, and providing your own content lowers the cost.
* **Functionality.** Contact forms are standard. Booking, online stores, and calculators each add development time.
* **SEO setup.** Titles, schema markup, a sitemap, fast performance, and local optimization take deliberate work.
* **Revisions.** More rounds of feedback mean more time, and a clear brief keeps this efficient.

## What Costs Come After Launch?

The build price is only part of the picture. Budget for these too:

* **Hosting:** $10 to $50 a month for most small business sites, sometimes bundled
* **Domain:** $12 to $25 a year
* **SSL certificate:** usually free now, sometimes bundled with hosting
* **Maintenance and updates:** platform updates, security patches, and content changes, which cost your time, a developer's hourly rate, or the monthly fee
* **Redesigns:** many one-time builds are replaced every three to five years

Try this exercise. Take the upfront price, add five years of every recurring cost, then divide by 60. That monthly number is the fairest way to compare a one-time build with a monthly plan.

## What Does a Five-Year Comparison Look Like?

For a typical Triangle service business with a 10 to 15 page site:

* **DIY builder:** about $30 a month, plus 40 or more hours of your time. Five-year cash cost near $1,800, with performance limits.
* **Freelancer:** about $3,500 up front, plus $50 a month. Five-year cost near $6,500, assuming no major redesign.
* **Agency one-time:** about $9,000 up front, plus a $150 a month care plan, before any redesign. Five-year cost near $18,000, and higher if the site is rebuilt in year four.
* **Monthly plan:** $0 down and $150 a month. Five-year cost $9,000, with updates and hosting included and no redesign invoice.

These figures are illustrative, not quotes. They show why the monthly number deserves as much attention as the headline price. For a platform comparison, read [hand-coded vs WordPress](/blog/hand-coded-vs-wordpress/).

## What Should You Ask Any Provider?

1. What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
2. Who owns the site and the domain, and can I take them elsewhere?
3. Is it hand-coded or built on a page builder, and how fast does it load?
4. Is local SEO setup included?
5. How are updates handled after launch, and what do they cost?
6. What happens in three years: do I need a redesign, and at what price?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a small business website cost?

The range runs from almost nothing for a do-it-yourself builder to well over ten thousand dollars for a custom agency build. The right figure depends on pages, features, and who maintains the site.

### What costs come after launch?

Hosting, security, updates, edits, and any support. These ongoing costs add up over several years, so compare the five-year total, not only the build price.

### Is a monthly website plan cheaper than a one-time build?

It costs less to start and spreads the cost over time. Over several years the totals can be close, so compare both with the ongoing costs included.

### What questions should I ask a web designer about price?

Ask what is included each month, how many pages are covered, who handles edits, what happens if you cancel, and who owns the site.
