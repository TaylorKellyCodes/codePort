"How much does a website cost" is one of the most common questions small business owners ask, and the honest answer is that it depends on the path you choose. The range in Raleigh runs from almost nothing for a do-it-yourself builder to well over ten thousand dollars for a custom agency build. What matters more than the sticker price is understanding what drives the cost and what you will keep paying after launch.

This breakdown covers every common option, what each one includes, and how to think about the total cost over three to five years rather than just the first invoice.

## The Four Ways to Get a Website

### 1. Do-It-Yourself Builders

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, and GoDaddy's builder let you assemble a site yourself from templates.

* **Upfront cost:** often $0 to start
* **Monthly cost:** roughly $16 to $50 for a business plan, billed forever
* **Your time:** significant, especially the first time
* **Best fit:** a brand-new business that needs a simple presence quickly and has more time than budget

The tradeoffs are performance and ceiling. Template builders load more code than most small sites need, which affects speed and can hold back local rankings. When you outgrow the template, your options for customization are limited.

### 2. Freelancers

An independent designer or developer builds a custom or semi-custom site.

* **Upfront cost:** commonly $1,500 to $6,000 for a small business site in the Triangle
* **Monthly cost:** hosting and maintenance, often $20 to $150, sometimes handled by you
* **Timeline:** three to eight weeks
* **Best fit:** a business that wants something tailored and has a clear scope

Quality and availability vary widely. The main thing to confirm is what happens after launch: who hosts it, who makes updates, and what those updates cost.

### 3. Agencies (One-Time Build)

A web design agency handles strategy, design, development, and often content.

* **Upfront cost:** commonly $5,000 to $25,000 and up for a small business site, more with custom features
* **Monthly cost:** a care plan, often $50 to $500
* **Timeline:** two to four months
* **Best fit:** an established business that needs a larger site, custom functionality, or a full brand treatment

You get a polished result and a team behind it. The considerations are the size of the upfront commitment and the redesign cycle: many agency sites are rebuilt every three to five years at a similar cost.

### 4. Monthly Plans (Subscription)

Some providers build a custom site with little or no upfront cost and charge a flat monthly fee that covers the build, hosting, and ongoing changes.

* **Upfront cost:** $0 down is common
* **Monthly cost:** typically $100 to $300, which folds in hosting, security, and edits
* **Timeline:** two to six weeks
* **Best fit:** a service business that would rather keep capital in the business and have updates handled without per-change invoices

At Triangle Web Design this is the model we use: a hand-coded site for **$0 down and $150 a month**, with hosting, security, and unlimited edits included. The value of a subscription approach is predictability. You know the number every month, and small changes do not turn into separate bills.

## What Actually Drives the Price

Whatever path you choose, these factors move the cost the most.

* **Number of pages.** A five-page site is far less work than a thirty-page site with dozens of service and location pages.
* **Custom design versus template.** A design built for your brand takes more time than adjusting a stock theme.
* **Content.** Writing the copy, sourcing or shooting photos, and building out service pages adds hours. Providing your own content lowers the cost.
* **Functionality.** Contact forms are standard. Online booking, e-commerce, membership areas, calculators, and integrations each add development time.
* **SEO setup.** Proper titles, metadata, schema markup, a sitemap, fast performance, and local optimization take deliberate work and are worth confirming are included.
* **Revisions.** More rounds of feedback mean more time. A clear brief keeps this efficient.

## The Costs That Come After Launch

The build price is only part of the picture. Budget for these too.

* **Hosting:** $10 to $50 a month for most small business sites, sometimes bundled
* **Domain:** $12 to $25 a year
* **SSL certificate:** usually free now, sometimes bundled with hosting
* **Maintenance and updates:** platform updates, plugin updates, security patches, and content changes. On a self-managed site this is your time or a developer's hourly rate. On a care plan or subscription it is the monthly fee.
* **Redesigns:** many one-time builds are replaced every three to five years. Spreading that cost across the period gives you the real annual figure.

A useful exercise: take the upfront price, add five years of every recurring cost, then divide by 60. That monthly number is the fairest way to compare a $6,000 one-time build against a $150 a month plan against a $30 a month DIY builder plus your time.

## A Realistic Five-Year Comparison

For a typical Triangle service business with a 10 to 15 page site:

* **DIY builder:** about $30 a month plan, plus 40 or more hours of your time up front and ongoing. Five-year cash cost near $1,800, with performance limits.
* **Freelancer:** about $3,500 up front, plus $50 a month for hosting and occasional updates. Five-year cost near $6,500, assuming no major redesign.
* **Agency one-time:** about $9,000 up front, plus a $150 a month care plan, plus a likely redesign near year four. Five-year cost near $20,000.
* **Monthly plan:** $0 down and $150 a month, all in. Five-year cost $9,000, with updates and hosting included and no redesign invoice.

These are illustrative, not quotes, but they show why the monthly figure matters more than the headline price.

## Questions to Ask Any Provider

1. What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
2. Who owns the site and the domain, and can I take them elsewhere?
3. Is it hand-coded or built on a page builder, and how fast does it load?
4. Is local SEO setup included: titles, schema, sitemap, speed, Google Business Profile guidance?
5. How are updates handled after launch, and what do they cost?
6. What happens in three years: do I need a redesign, and at what price?

## The Bottom Line for Raleigh Small Businesses

A small business website in the Triangle can cost anywhere from a couple hundred dollars a year to twenty thousand or more over five years. The right number depends on how large the site is, how custom the design needs to be, and how you prefer to handle the cost: a large payment up front, or a predictable monthly fee with everything included.
