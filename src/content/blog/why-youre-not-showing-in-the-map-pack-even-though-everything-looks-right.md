If your business is missing from the Google Map Pack, the cause is usually a problem with the listing itself, not a shortage of reviews. The usual suspects are a suspended profile, a duplicate listing, mismatched business details, an oversized service area, or the proximity filter. Each has a fix that takes hours, not months.

Start by working out which of two situations you are in, because they need different fixes.

## Are You Invisible or Just Not Ranking?

You are invisible if you search your exact business name in an incognito window and nothing comes up, or the result is not yours. That points to a problem at the account level: a suspension, a duplicate listing, or a profile stuck in review.

You are visible but not ranking if your business appears for its own name but never reaches the top three for the searches that bring in customers. That is a relevance and prominence problem. Our guide to [Map Pack ranking factors](/blog/seo-google-map-pack-ranking-factors/) covers it.

Rule out the first situation before you spend months on reviews and blog posts. Many owners chase ranking advice when the real issue is a broken listing.

## Is Your Google Business Profile Suspended?

A suspended profile does not appear in the Map Pack, whatever else you do. Log in to your Google Business Profile manager and look for a notice at the top of the dashboard.

The most common triggers for local service businesses are:

* A business name stuffed with keywords, such as "Smith Plumbing | Emergency Plumber Raleigh"
* A virtual office or PO box used as the physical address
* Repeated edits to the category or service list in a short time

To get reinstated, file a request with proof the business is real: a utility bill for the address, a business document, or photos of your vehicle or signage. Review times vary, and owners report anything from several days to several weeks. Read Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) before you file, so your request matches what they check.

## Do Duplicate Listings or Mismatched Details Hold You Back?

Yes, both can. A second, unofficial listing splits reviews, clicks, and trust between two profiles, and neither becomes strong enough to rank. Duplicates often come from an old address, a former employee, or a directory that generated a profile on its own.

Search your business name on Google Maps and check every result, not only the first. If you can claim the duplicate, claim it and ask Google to merge the two. If you cannot, report it as a duplicate and point to your verified listing.

Google also compares your name, address, and phone number against Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places, and your website. Small differences add up: "St" in one place and "Street" in another, an old phone number on a forgotten listing, a suite number that appears in only some places. Compare five or six directories against your profile line by line. Our guide to [local citations and NAP consistency](/blog/seo-local-citations-nap-consistency/) walks through the full audit.

## Is Your Service Area Bigger Than Your Real Work?

Service-area businesses go to the customer instead of running a storefront, so they set a service area on the profile. Problems start when that area is unrealistic. A landscaper listing three counties with no real jobs in most of them looks like an exaggeration, and Google compares the area you claim with your actual activity.

An oversized service area can get a profile filtered, not just ignored. Pull it back to the places where your jobs happen. A business that ranks in its real territory, such as [Raleigh](/service-areas/raleigh/) or [Durham](/service-areas/durham/), beats one that is filtered out of a territory it never served.

## What Is the Proximity Filter?

The proximity filter is a decluttering feature that stops the map from showing several nearly identical businesses at the same address. If your listing appears when you zoom out on Google Maps and disappears as you zoom in on your area, this is the likely cause.

It triggers most often when several businesses in one building share the same primary category, such as a shared office or a strip mall. The fastest fix is a more specific primary category than your neighbors use. If that does not fit your business, a different physical address is the longer-term fix.

## How Long Does a New or Edited Profile Take to Show?

A freshly verified profile, or one with a batch of recent edits, often needs a few weeks of normal activity before it settles into stable rankings. Normal activity means photos going up, posts going out, and a few new reviews coming in.

During this window the right move is to wait and keep up the routine work. Repeated edits can reset the clock, so resist the urge to tweak the profile every few days.

## Can a Weak Website Keep You Out of the Map Pack?

Yes. Your profile does most of the work, but Google says prominence also draws on information from across the web, such as how many sites link to you. A slow, vague, or thin website gives Google less to support what your profile claims.

Check that your site names your services and service area, loads quickly on a phone, and shows the same name, address, and phone number as your profile. ## A Quick Diagnostic Checklist

Work through these steps in order:

1. Search your exact business name in an incognito window. Nothing showing means something structural is broken.
2. Check your profile dashboard for a suspension notice.
3. Search your business name on Maps and look for a second listing.
4. Compare your name, address, and phone number across five directories.
5. Zoom slowly into your location on Maps to check for the proximity filter.
6. Note any recent edits or verification, since either can explain a temporary dip.

If everything checks out and you still sit outside the top three, you have a ranking problem, not a listing problem. Our [Google Business Profile guide for contractors](/blog/seo-google-business-profile-tips-contractors/) goes deeper on the profile side.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my business not showing in the Google Map Pack?

The common causes are a suspended profile, a duplicate listing, mismatched business details, an oversized service area, or the proximity filter. Check those before you work on rankings.

### How do I know if my Google Business Profile is suspended?

Log in to your profile manager and look for a notice at the top of the dashboard. A suspended profile does not appear in the Map Pack.

### What is the proximity filter?

It is a Google feature that avoids showing several nearly identical businesses from one address. It hides some listings when you zoom in on an area.

### How long does a new Google Business Profile take to rank?

Expect a few weeks of normal activity after verification or a batch of edits before rankings settle.

### Can my website stop me from showing in the Map Pack?

A thin or slow website can weaken an otherwise good profile, because Google uses web search standing as part of local ranking.
